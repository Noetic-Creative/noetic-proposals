01. The quarter in sequence

Three months, four phases, one measurable funnel.

01
May · Discovery and audit

Understanding the machine before touching it

Kickoff on May 19 with Ben, Adam and Mallory. Full product walkthrough, then parallel audits across paid search, email and organic social. Two priorities surfaced immediately: a post trial nurture sequence that did not yet exist, and paid search campaigns where branded terms were absorbing credit for traffic that was arriving through other routes.

02
Early June · Strategy

A position, not a campaign

The strategy session on June 4 reset the foundation. Claudine pushed the team to recognize that the economic buyer sits in the C suite rather than in the HR function, and that a soft hiring market makes workforce planning and pay visibility the more urgent story. Compensation conviction became the organizing idea, and every content pillar was rebuilt around it.

03
Mid June · Instrumentation

Making the funnel visible

Before optimization could mean anything, the measurement layer had to be repaired. Custom conversion events built in GA4 and imported into Google Ads, tag coverage extended across paid destinations, and the CRM handoff between HubSpot and Salesforce reconciled so a lead could be followed from click to outcome.

04
Late June to July · Execution

Contraction, then compounding

Performance Max and Demand Gen paused, budget concentrated on search intent, keyword and negative keyword sets rebuilt. The monthly media budget of $1,500 was restructured into a deliberate split of 50 percent Non-Brand Search, 20 percent Brand Search and 30 percent Display Remarketing, with the paused campaign budget reallocated across those three. In parallel, the organic calendar went live across LinkedIn and Meta, the Pay Analysis launch campaign was produced, and the free trial email sequence was written, designed and built.

04. A note on attribution

Every number in this report is a confirmed minimum.

During this quarter a serious attribution problem was identified across the measurement stack. We are confident that a meaningful number of leads generated in this period are not represented in the figures above, because the systems that should have recorded them were not connected end to end. This is stated plainly rather than quietly, because it changes how the results should be read.

/ 01

A lead inside this report has no traceable record

Of the eight conversions captured, one cannot be located in the CRM at all. The gap is visible inside the reporting data itself, which is the clearest possible evidence that the capture layer was incomplete.

/ 02

Sales confirms activity this report does not show

The LaborIQ sales team has confirmed that more trial activity has occurred than appears in the tracked data, and that other engagement is not being captured. Their direct observation is independent corroboration of the same gap.

/ 03

Three systems, three different counts

For the same period, the ad platform reports approximately 9 Schedule Demo and 3 Free Trial conversions. The sales tracker records 5 Demo and 3 Trial. Until these reconcile, no single source can be treated as complete.

/ 04

Tag coverage was missing on paid destinations

Container code was absent from a set of landing pages receiving paid traffic. Any conversion occurring on those pages during that window was invisible to reporting, regardless of whether the visitor converted.

/ 05

A newsletter form was absorbing conversion credit

A signup form in the site footer was capturing submissions on pages that display campaigns were driving to, attributing intent to the wrong action and masking the true performance of those campaigns.

/ 06

Record ownership did not map between systems

Owner records in the marketing platform had no matching counterpart in the CRM, which prevented leads from being created and assigned cleanly. Access to resolve this at source is the remaining piece.

The instrumentation work completed this quarter closes most of these gaps, so next quarter's reporting won't just look better — it will be truer.

WORKSTREAM / 01

Measurement infrastructure

The unglamorous work that makes every other number possible.

A significant share of the quarter went into repairing the layer beneath the campaigns. None of it is visible in a creative review, but all of it determines whether the reporting can be trusted.

  • Corrected the account's primary conversion goals so Demo and Free Trial are what the account optimizes toward, and fixed the Free Trial event to fire on the actual confirmation step
  • Built a dedicated free trial conversion event in GA4 and imported it into Google Ads so bidding could optimize toward the action that matters
  • Audited tag coverage across every paid destination and identified the pages running without container code
  • Traced the newsletter footer form that was absorbing conversion credit from display campaigns
  • Reconciled record ownership between the marketing platform and the CRM to unblock clean lead creation
  • Mapped form field to CRM property coverage and documented the fields that were not being written through
  • Established the shared lead quality tracker that carries sales outcomes back into media optimization
Featured · Conversion tracking rebuild
From three answers to one
GA4 · Google Ads · HubSpot · Salesforce
The situation

At the start of the engagement the same question, how many leads did we generate, returned a different answer depending on which system was asked. Conversion events were incomplete, tag coverage was partial, and the handoff between marketing automation and the CRM did not carry ownership cleanly.

What was done

The account's conversion goals were pointed at a generic lead form and a newsletter signup. Both have been replaced:

  • Demo and Free Trial set as the two actions the account now optimizes toward
  • The Free Trial event fixed to fire on the actual confirmation step
  • Tag gaps located and documented across paid destinations
  • Competing forms traced to source
  • The lead record path mapped end to end, from click to sales outcome
Why the change matters

The account was already producing roughly 10 conversions a month before this work. It was simply counting the wrong actions. That same volume now represents Demo and Free Trial intent, which means the bidding algorithm is finally being trained on the outcomes the business actually cares about.

05. Lifecycle and email

A database of this size is an asset in waiting.

The email audit produced the single largest opportunity identified this quarter. It also produced the clearest path to acting on it.

Audit · Marketing database

190,784 contacts

A complete audit of the marketing database, its segmentation, its sending history and its engagement profile over the trailing twelve months.

HubSpot Deliverability Segmentation Lifecycle

What the audit found

Of 190,784 total contacts, 153,496 are classified as marketing contacts. Across the past year, 1.2% of them opened an email. The reach is already owned and already paid for. What has been missing is a reason to open and a rhythm to expect.

Why this is the opportunity, not the problem

Low engagement across a large, already-owned audience is a very different problem than having no audience at all — the list, the permission and the sending infrastructure already exist. Moving engagement even fractionally against a base this size would outproduce any paid channel at comparable cost.

The wider sending picture

Since January 2024 the account has sent 165 emails, roughly 148,000 total sends, for a 26.5 percent open rate and a 1.19 percent click rate — proof the sender reputation and the creative both work. Set against the 1.2 percent annual engagement figure, the pattern is clear: sending has stayed concentrated on a small, responsive repeat segment while most of the database has gone quiet.

Build · Free trial nurture

The sequence that did not exist

A seven email program, complete and ready to launch, designed to carry a trial user from first login through to a decision call, running alongside the platform's own transactional sends rather than competing with them.

HubSpot sequences Plain text Branded lifecycle Rescue branch

The architecture

Seven emails, timed against each individual's signup date rather than a calendar, and sent from a named person at LaborIQ rather than a brand:

  • Day 0–1 — first value and activation
  • Days 3, 8 and 13 — use case depth
  • Day 15 — pre-expiry decision prompt
  • Days 18 and 25 — follow-up
  • Rescue branch — a separate track for signups who haven't activated, so a stalled account gets a different message than an engaged one

Built around where trials actually drop off

The cadence isn't evenly spaced: it concentrates around the points where trial engagement has historically fallen away, weighting the sequence's middle stretch heaviest. It also splits into two registers — branded, designed sends carrying product and educational content, and the plain-text trial sequence running from a named representative — with a structured test on the first trial email confirming that split against live data.

Supporting work delivered

Supporting work is complete:

  • A full newsletter and email design system built in Figma, rebuilt for email-client rendering so gradients and backgrounds survive Outlook
  • Copy for all seven sends written, designed and complete
  • An unfired template variable in the platform footer identified and flagged ahead of trial volume increasing

What it is ready to support

The sequence exists to convert trial signups into decision conversations. Set against the account value established earlier in this report, roughly $32,000 in lifetime value per converted account, a single additional conversion across the trial base repays the build many times over.

06. Where the next gains sit

The ads are working. The destination is the constraint.

Everything below is drawn from live performance data rather than from best practice. Each item is a specific, identified friction point between a qualified visitor and a completed action.

/ 01

The trial form asks for eleven or more fields

Name, company, job title, phone, website, full street address, city, state and postcode are collected before a trial can begin. Each additional field costs completions, and address in particular is an unusual ask at this stage of a software evaluation.

/ 02

Verification requires leaving the form

After completing the fields, the visitor receives a code and must retrieve it from a separate inbox before returning to finish. Any step that requires leaving the page introduces a point where a genuinely interested visitor simply does not come back.

/ 03

A two step form preserves the data and the lead

Splitting collection means the essentials are captured first and the enrichment fields follow. Crucially, a visitor who abandons partway through has still been captured as a lead, where today that visitor is lost entirely.

/ 04

Landing page experience scores below average

Across non branded keywords the platform rates ad relevance above average and landing page experience below it. That gap raises cost per click and lowers conversion probability at the same time, so it works against the account twice.

/ 05

Competing calls to action sit on conversion pages

Newsletter signup forms and full site navigation remain on pages receiving paid traffic. Every alternative path offered on a conversion page is a path away from the conversion the visit was paid for.

/ 06

Some ad promises do not match their destination

One tested ad offered a fifteen minute review and routed to a demo request page. Where the promise and the page diverge, engagement drops immediately, and the fix costs nothing beyond alignment.
