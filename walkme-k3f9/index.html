SERVICE / 01

Strategy, pillars, and the calendar

The calendar is the artifact. The system is the reason it works.

Most social calendars are a list of things to post. Ours is a map of things to prove. We start from what the brand needs the market to believe, turn that into four to six pillars, and tag every post to one. If a post has no pillar, it has no reason.

  • Channel and competitive audit in week one, with an honest read on what is working
  • Messaging architecture: pillars, proof points, and voice guardrails
  • Calendar locked a month ahead, one approval cycle, no weekly scramble
  • Format mix planned across text, carousel, video, and document
  • Monthly read that changes next month's calendar, not just reports on last month's

05 — Freelancer or team

An honest comparison, including the part where we are the harder option.

A good freelancer is a reasonable answer to a six-month gap and we will not pretend otherwise. But the two options are not the same product, and you should choose knowing exactly what you are trading.

Option A
A freelancer
Option B
An organic social team
Depth
Trade-offOne skill set stretched across strategy, copy, design, video, and community. Whichever is their weakest becomes your channel's weakest.
StrengthA strategist writes strategy, a copywriter writes copy, a designer designs, an editor cuts, a community manager engages. Nobody is doing the thing they are worst at.
Continuity
Trade-offSingle point of failure. Illness, a better offer, a competing client, or a two-week holiday, and the channel simply stops.
StrengthRedundancy is built in. Someone is always on. The account does not have a heartbeat that can flatline.
Strategic planning
Trade-offMost freelancers are hired to execute, and they execute well. Strategy is usually the thing that quietly does not happen, because there is no time left for it.
StrengthA dedicated strategic planner and a monthly read that changes the next calendar. Planning is somebody's job, not somebody's evening.
Production
Trade-offVideo, motion, and design get subcontracted or skipped. Ambition gets sized to what one person can make on a Thursday.
StrengthIn-house design, motion, and video. Shoot days, executive interviews, creator production. What we can imagine and what we can ship are the same list.
Community
Trade-offPublishing consumes the day. Comments, DMs, and practitioner threads get whatever is left, which is usually nothing.
StrengthDedicated community managers. Daily engagement is a role with a name attached, not a nice-to-have at the bottom of a list.
Cost
StrengthLower and simpler. This is a real advantage and it would be dishonest to pretend it is not.
Trade-offA team costs more than a person. What you are buying is capability and continuity, and it should be judged on whether those are worth it to you.
Time to first post
StrengthFast. Hand over the logins and they are posting this week.
Trade-offSlower off the line. We spend the first two weeks learning your brand, your legal constraints, and your buyers before we publish.
The handback
Trade-offKnowledge leaves with the person. The returning owner inherits a login and a spreadsheet.
StrengthDocumented system, organized asset library, live creator relationships, working calendar. She comes back to an asset, not a backlog.
Where we are weaker

We will be slower to start. A freelancer is posting in week one and we will not be. We use those first two weeks on the audit, the messaging system, and the calendar, because everything after week three depends on getting them right. If what you need is somebody posting on Monday, hire the freelancer. If what you need is the channel to be materially better in six months, the two weeks are the price.
