After Nurture

What happens when a nurture flow ends and nobody has booked a demo.

Forecast model, figures projected from current flow behaviour · revised 2 Sept working session

THE GOAL

Every exit is a next step.

Two goals, in order. First, every contact leaving Nurture A or B lands in one of two paths instead of stopping cold. Second, the program produces the engagement data Opmed does not have yet, so next quarter gets planned on measurement, not on estimate.

A routing decision and a measurement layer, built at the same time.

THE DECISION

Two paths. Clicks decide, not opens.

🤝

Engaged goes to a human

A click on a demo CTA, two clicks across the flow, or a second form submission. These contacts get a rep, not another email.

🔄

Quiet goes to a reset

No clicks means the message is not landing. A short three email flow asks a different question instead of repeating the same one.

Clicks and form submissions only. Opens stay out of the rule until deliverability supports them.

THE RULE

Four exits. Every contact lands in one.

⭐

TIER 1

Sales Ready

Clicked a demo CTA, or clicked twice across the flow, or submitted a second form.

→ Hand to rep
🌤️

TIER 2

Warm

Exactly one click, and not on a demo CTA. Interested, but not asking.

→ Rep if capacity, else reset
💤

TIER 3

Passive

Opens recorded across the flow, but no click anywhere.

→ Re-engagement flow
🗄️

TIER 4

Dormant

No engagement recorded at any point in the sequence.

→ Suppress, quarterly

Decided on the call

Tier 1 and Tier 2 do not get their own email track. Their message carries a sales rep's name and reply-to, so it reads as SQL-ready. That is email-level personalization only, not a Salesforce status change. The actual SQL handoff still runs through the lead-score-based process Daniela's team already built with Salesforce, and this sits alongside it, not in place of it.

Any hard bounce or unsubscribe exits the program entirely, at any point.

RE-ENGAGEMENT, TIER 3 ONLY

Three emails. One question, asked once.

This is not more nurture. It is a permission and timing reset for Tier 3 contacts specifically, people who opened something in the flow but never clicked. It starts 30 days after the nurture exit so the contact gets a genuine gap, and it runs 60 days from first email to last.

  1. 01.Still the right time?Day 30, captures a timing signal
  2. 02.One proof pointDay 60, strongest single asset
  3. 03.Permission closeDay 90, a clean yes or no

Decided on the call

Three emails, not the fuller version with a separate message per nurture email someone opened. That fits inside the client's cap of up to 4 marketing emails a month, and it folds into the Warm Lead Score nurture cadence rather than shipping as a standalone flow. The per-opened-email variant is a real idea, saved for a later phase once volume justifies it.

Each delay carries a delay-status branch, so late joiners exit rather than receive a stale email.

THE HANDOFF

One rule: a click creates a task.

💬

Notify through HubSpot, task in Salesforce

When a Tier 1 contact acts, HubSpot sends a notification that opens a call task in Salesforce, so the moment is not lost. The exact mechanism, a native workflow action versus a Slack relay, still needs a technical check. Slack is the fallback path either way, since it sidesteps the seat gap on the two largest Salesforce-synced contact owners, who hold no HubSpot seat at all.

⏱️

Put a clock on it

Alert to rep touch within 24 business hours. If Lead Status has not moved in three days, the contact drops automatically into Tier 2 treatment. At 15 to 30 Tier 1 contacts a quarter, every one of them can be worked personally.

Raised on the call, not yet a decision

Filter the Tier 3 and Tier 4 lists by existing lead score before any send, and route anyone who takes an after-nurture action straight to a rep with that score attached. Worth doing, needs a yes before it ships.
