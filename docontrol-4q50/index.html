01 / What we discussed

A product that wins on contact, a company consolidating marketing, and a list already written.

  • /01

    Three brands, one company.

    Spin.AI is the parent: SaaS backup for Google Workspace, Salesforce, Slack and Microsoft, with roughly 1,500 customers across every size band. Revyz, acquired by Spin, brings the same backup to the Atlassian suite and runs on its own sales motion. DoControl is the security brand: DLP and CASB, API-only, no agent, with Spin's browser security product folding under it and sold together. The DoControl name stays, and the sentiment attached to it is strong.

  • /02

    Marketing is consolidating under you.

    Over the next month-plus you're taking over marketing for Spin, budgets included. Today it runs in pieces: a small function on the DoControl side, a VP Marketing at Spin with designers and SDRs, and partners bringing most of the new business. You intend to reorganize, centralize, and run it as one function with one plan.

  • /03

    The product does the closing.

    The free risk assessment converts at a rate most vendors would not believe. The demo has a wow factor: files shared to personal accounts, documents publicly reachable for years, ex-employees still inside the Drive, all cross-referenced with the HRIS and Okta, then remediated at scale with workflow automation. The hard part is upstream. The category is not yet understood, and the first reaction is often "why would I worry about my Google Workspace?"

  • /04

    The ICP is already defined.

    Companies of 250 employees and up, with a security function in place (a CISO, an information security manager, an analyst), on Google Workspace or a comparable stack. You hold a list of about 3,000 such companies with buying committees mapped, and you're sending it over. Smaller companies are not the target: no budget, no owner, security not yet in the mindset.

  • /05

    What you asked us for.

    A bundle rather than a single service: SEO, paid search on the brand terms of DoControl and Spin, lead generation, and a brand awareness layer for the accounts not yet searching. First a document that shows we understand the three-brand company. Then a proposal with services, channels, KPIs, time-to-value, and a measurement model you can defend internally: UTMs, fingerprinting, and a clean line back to HubSpot. One enterprise customer traceable to this work is the proof point you named.

  • /06

    Where things stand.

    SEO runs in-house today and ranks on the keywords you chose. Newsletters and in-product cross-sell to Spin's customer base already exist. Marketing lives in HubSpot; sales is moving to Salesforce, marketing stays put. You floated a November start with a six-month budget and KPIs attached.

    Agreed actions: you send the ICP list and field questions by email. We run an SEO and AI visibility audit on DoControl, return with this brief, and follow with the proposal.

03 / Three postures, three messages

You described three kinds of CISO. Each one needs a different first sentence.

The same demo closes all three. What changes is what gets them to book it. This is the segmentation the content plan is built on.

The one who'd rather not look

Knows the Drive is a mess, suspects the audit would be painful, and chooses not to open the door. The most common posture, by your account.

What moves themLower the cost of looking. The assessment is free, agentless, and takes minutes. Lead with peers who looked and survived, not with fear.

The one with a known problem

Something already happened, or a board asked a question. Actively evaluating, typing "Google Workspace security" or "Slack DLP" into a search bar this quarter.

What moves themBe there when they search. Own the category terms, defend the brand terms, and make the path from result to assessment one click long.

The one who thinks it's covered

Has a full stack of well-known endpoint and network tools and assumes SaaS sharing is inside their perimeter. It is not, and the gap is invisible until someone shows it.

What moves themPrecision, not volume. Named-account content that names the gap: what the existing stack sees, what it does not, and what a scan of their own instance would surface.

05 / Capabilities

CAPABILITY / 01

Account-Based Marketing

Three thousand accounts is a list. A program is what you do with it.

You already have the hardest part: a defined ICP and a named list with buying committees. Our job is to turn it into tiers, personas, and content that gives each person on the committee a reason to care, then to push every signal back into HubSpot so sales calls the warm ones first.

  • Tier the 3,000 into priority bands; enrich with ZoomInfo and Apollo to fill the committee where it's thin
  • Map the committee per account: the security champion, the operations owner, the finance signer
  • Build content per persona and per posture: guides, benchmarks, a "what's exposed in a company your size" calculator
  • Persona-level delivery on LinkedIn, plus Influ2 for the accounts that matter most
  • Engagement signals into HubSpot as lead scores, so the SDRs work a ranked list, not a flat one

CAPABILITY / 02

SEO & AI Visibility

You rank on the terms you chose. The question is what the searchers and the models see next.

SEO now runs on two tracks. The classic one, on-page performance and content architecture, still decides who wins the searchers you described in posture two. The second is AI visibility: being the source the models cite when someone asks a chatbot how to secure Google Workspace. That is built on citable, specific, well-referenced content published where the models read, Reddit included. You asked whether we do backlinks and Reddit. Yes, and in the open, under real names.

  • Audit first: technical health, current rankings, content gaps against category and competitor terms, and how DoControl appears in AI answers today
  • Topic clusters around the buyer's questions: SaaS DLP, Google Workspace security, Slack and Drive sharing risk, offboarding exposure
  • Citable content: benchmarks, definitions, data from your own scans (anonymized), written the way models pick up
  • Reddit and community presence handled by people who disclose who they are and answer well
  • Authority building: backlinks earned through real assets, not bought

CAPABILITY / 04

HubSpot & Lifecycle

The signal is the product of everything above. HubSpot is where it has to land.

Marketing stays on HubSpot while sales moves to Salesforce, which makes the handoff the most important integration in the program. Every engagement from ABM, search, and paid becomes a score, a segment, and a nurture path. The measurement model you asked for, UTMs, fingerprinting, and attribution you can show internally, is built here, not bolted on later.

  • Attribution architecture: UTM governance, first- and multi-touch models, a dashboard that shows which account came from where
  • Lead scoring from engagement signals, so "watched the calculator, downloaded the benchmark, visited pricing" means something
  • Nurture tracks per posture and per persona, each ending at the assessment
  • The HubSpot-to-Salesforce sync designed so sales sees the marketing context, not just a contact record
  • Cross-sell flows for Spin's existing base: backup customers who are also DoControl prospects
Featured · Segmentation, nurture, dashboards

CAPABILITY / 05

UGC & Creative

The football clip worked because it didn't look like a security vendor made it. Keep that.

Awareness to a list of 3,000 lives or dies on creative. A CISO scrolls past brand video; they stop for a person who sounds like them. We run UGC as a production line: casting real practitioners and creators, briefing them with scene-by-scene scripts, filming on their own devices, editing in-house, and refreshing monthly so the same account never sees the same ad twice. Everything produced is yours, for paid, for organic, for email.

  • Casting: security practitioners, IT admins and creators who can talk about shared drives without reading a script
  • Scene-level briefs per posture: "I'd rather not look," "we have a problem," "we thought we were covered"
  • Two lanes: creator-led UGC for LinkedIn and Meta, and brand-led explainers cut from the same footage
  • Monthly refresh cadence tied to what the media team sees performing
  • Eight people in creative and design, plus video editing, in-house
Reference · UGC production line
Rail Europe
Consumer travel · Creator program
Challenge

Brand video was slow, expensive and stiff. Social audiences burned through it fast, and creative freshness was the biggest lever on cost per booking.

The audience doesn't reward polish. It rewards a person they believe. Once the pipeline was in place, fresh creative stopped being the bottleneck.
