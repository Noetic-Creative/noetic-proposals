01 — The setup

Built for marketing. Now asked to sell.

The system we shipped in the spring did what the brief asked. Forms feed HubSpot. Lifecycle stages move. Segments update themselves. Automations fire without anyone watching. It was designed, on purpose, around marketing: where a lead came from, what it did, and when it was ready for a conversation.

Then the sales side of Agwa changed. New leadership, a new commercial motion, and a fresh pair of eyes on the deal pipeline. What Xerxes found is what most fast-moving companies find twelve months into a CRM: the pipeline describes how deals were supposed to move, not how they move now. Eight stages, several of them without a weighting, and one blended funnel where a single-cabinet pilot and a multi-vessel fleet rollout sit in the same column and can never be told apart.

That is not a failure of the original build. It is the natural next step. A marketing system gets built first because it has to be. A sales system gets built second, once the company knows exactly how it closes. Agwa knows that now.

Three pipelines, each shaped like a real Agwa deal. Two ways to get there.

What Xerxes asked for is a specific change with a specific scope: re-architect the pipelines, rewire everything that depended on the old stages, migrate the existing deals, and make the company record the source of truth for where an account stands. Option A does exactly that, as a fixed-scope project for a fixed fee.

But this is the second time in six months that Agwa's HubSpot has needed to change shape, and it will not be the last. New hires, new segments, a renewal cohort arriving for the first time, an integration that suddenly matters. Option B is the alternative: a six-month retainer that includes the same expansion in its first month, then keeps a HubSpot specialist on the account for whatever comes next. It is one or the other. Pick the one that matches how much change you expect.

04 — What we heard

Six things that came out of the walkthrough.

Daniela sat with Xerxes and walked through the system as built, section by section. These are the six changes that surfaced. They are the spine of both options, and the pipeline structure above came directly out of them.

  • /01

    The pipeline has more stages than the sales motion does

    Eight stages, two of them with no weighting at all, all in one pipeline. Most of them are not needed for the way Agwa sells today. The ask is fewer stages, split into three pipelines that each reflect a distinct kind of deal: the pilot, the fleet rollout, and the renewal.

  • /02

    Everything downstream is wired to the old stages

    Automations, lifecycle triggers, reporting, forecast weightings. All of it references the current stage names. Changing the pipeline is the easy part; rewiring what depends on it is the real work, and it has to happen in the same window so nothing breaks in between.

  • /03

    Roughly 300 existing deals need a new home

    Every open and closed deal has to be reviewed, classified by type, and placed into the right pipeline at the right stage. This is a data-quality exercise as much as a migration; it is the moment to clean what has accumulated.

  • /04

    The company, not the contact, is the unit that matters

    The contact-level lifecycle and lead-status sync works fine. What Xerxes wants to see is where the company stands, driven by its associated deals. That sync needs to be reviewed and, where it does not exist yet, built.

  • /05

    The ad platform connections have lapsed

    The Meta and LinkedIn ad account connections to HubSpot have expired. The person who originally authorized them is no longer at Agwa, so every ad account needs to be reconnected under current access, with attribution verified end to end afterward.

  • /06

    The center of gravity should move from marketing to sales enablement

    The system as built is, by design, marketing-first. The next phase should make HubSpot the place the sales team actually works from: the right views, the right alerts, the right numbers, and nothing that gets in the way of closing.

Architecture
01

Three-pipeline architecture with stage weightings

Build Pilot Sale, Fleet Rollout and Renewal & Upsell as separate HubSpot pipelines with the exact stages and weightings from the sales strategy review. Define entry and exit criteria per stage, required properties on stage change, and which pipeline a new deal lands in by default.

  • Pilot Sale: Initial Discussion 0% → Discovery 15% → Proposal 40% → Negotiation 65% → Closed 100%
  • Fleet Rollout: Proposal 25% → Negotiation 50% → Finalization 75% → Closed 100%
  • Renewal & Upsell: 120/90/60 Days Out 20% → In Progress 50% → Upsell Identified 70% → Closed 100%
  • Old pipeline retired only after migration is verified, so nothing goes dark in between
02

Pilot-to-fleet and renewal triggers

Pilot execution stays in Monday and Slack, where Customer Success runs it. HubSpot only needs one signal back. We build a "pilot success" property on the company record and the automation that opens a Fleet Rollout deal when it flips. A second automation opens a Renewal & Upsell deal 120 days before a contract end date.

Decision for Agwa: who flips the pilot-success flag, and whether it is set manually in HubSpot or pushed from Monday. Both are supported; the manual version ships first and can be automated later.
Rewiring
03

Automation, lifecycle and reporting rebuild

Every workflow, lifecycle trigger, notification and report that references an old stage gets audited and rebuilt against the new structure. This is the part that is invisible when it goes right and very visible when it does not.

  • Full inventory of existing automations and their stage dependencies, delivered as a one-page audit before changes begin
  • Workflows re-pointed to the new pipelines and stages, with pipeline-aware branching where a pilot and a fleet deal need different handling
  • Deal-stage notifications and SLA alerts rebuilt for the sales team, not the marketing team
  • Existing dashboards updated; forecast reporting reconfigured to use the new weightings
04

Deal migration and cleanup (~300 deals)

Every existing deal reviewed, classified by type, and moved to the correct pipeline and stage. Duplicates merged, dead deals closed out with a reason, missing amounts and close dates flagged for the sales team. A before-and-after count so you can see exactly what moved where.

  • Classification rules agreed with Xerxes up front, then applied consistently
  • Migration run in a controlled batch with a rollback path
  • Exceptions list for anything that needs a human call, kept short
Data and connections
05

Company-stage sync driven by associated deals

The contact-level lifecycle and lead-status sync stays as is. The new work is at the company level: a company stage that is set automatically from the most advanced associated deal across the three pipelines, so a company shows as Pilot, Fleet or Renewal without anyone updating it by hand. We review what exists, keep what works, and build what is missing.

06

Ad account reconnection and attribution check

Reconnect the Meta and LinkedIn ad accounts to HubSpot under Xerxes' access, since the original authorizing user has left Agwa. Then verify that lead-ad forms, UTM capture and source attribution flow end to end again, and document which user owns each connection so this does not repeat.

Sales enablement and handoff
07

Sales-first views, dashboard and training

Reorient the day-to-day experience around the people closing deals. A pipeline board per pipeline, saved views for the sales team, a sales dashboard with pilot-to-fleet conversion, weighted forecast by pipeline, and renewal exposure by month. Two training sessions: one for Xerxes on the system, one for the sales team on working the pipelines. Thirty days of async support after go-live.

  • One-page "how the pipelines work" doc for the next person who joins Agwa
  • Automation inventory handed over as a living document
