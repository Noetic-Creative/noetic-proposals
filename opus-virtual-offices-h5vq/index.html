01 · What we discussed

The goal is new clients. The question is where the next ones come from.

Six things we heard

You described the priority plainly: new client orders, then expansion of existing accounts, then recurring orders. New clients are the main measure.

Clients arrive through referrals, affiliates, organic search, maps and paid search on Google and Microsoft. Everything is run in-house except paid search. You noted that Google is not returning what it used to, and that advertising inside ChatGPT is a recent test that is still early.

Cancellations tend to cluster at about eleven to twelve months. The reasons you listed: businesses that close, clients who receive less mail or use the service less than they expected, and some who sign up mainly for the Google location and then leave.

Consultants and other professional services come first, followed by transportation: drivers, trucking companies and couriers. Law firms are valued for longevity and for opening offices across a state. Your team recently approved ads aimed at trucking and home services, and you noted that earlier campaigns aimed at trades such as electricians did not perform as hoped.

You have run webinars with SCORE for new entrepreneurs. The lesson from them was that a lot of people do not know what a virtual office is, how it helps, or what to search for. Your example was the blogger working from home who would rather not have her affiliate checks sent to her house.

Today the program includes a five-email sequence for people who start a signup and do not finish, plus bulk sends to past clients. We agreed email and lifecycle is an area with a lot of opportunity.

What we agreed

An audit and strategy presentation is the next step.

With access to the email platform, Google Ads and analytics, Noetic takes one to two weeks to audit the current activity and come back with a plan, with no commitment on your side.

Scott brings it to the CEO first.

Access to systems needs his approval. Nothing starts before that. Working model, scope and cost were left open for the proposal that follows the audit.

Paid Search

One search, one ad, one page.

You asked how this would work in practice, so here it is. In paid search the biggest lever is personalization. Every meaningful search gets its own ad and its own landing page, and traffic never lands on the homepage.

  • Keyword groups built around intent: by city, by service (address, mail, phone answering) and by audience.
  • Dedicated pages for comparison searches, where someone is weighing Opus against a larger name.
  • Landing pages produced at volume. We can build and launch them, or design in Figma for your IT team to implement.
  • Conversion tracking tied to a completed signup, so budgets follow orders and not clicks.
  • Newer placements, such as ads inside AI assistants, run as contained experiments with their own budget line.

Segment Creative & Paid Social

One product. A different first sentence for every audience.

Creative is the biggest lever in performance marketing. For Opus that means each audience sees its own pain point first, and the product second. We also set up the tracking behind it, so an ad is judged on signups and not impressions.

  • A message map per audience: the pain, the proof, the offer. For example, “Five cities. One attorney. Zero leases.” or “The truck moves. The address doesn't.”
  • Motion and video ads with strong opening hooks, designed in-house.
  • Creator content (UGC) from real business owners, where social proof carries the message.
  • Value-first formats, such as a short quiz that gives a business owner something useful before asking for anything.
  • Pixels, conversion events and audience structure in place before spend, so we can tell whether a message missed or the targeting did.

Email & Lifecycle

The work that happens after the signup.

Opus already has the foundations: a signup recovery sequence and a list of past clients. Lifecycle work turns those into a system that follows each client from the first unfinished form to the first renewal. The audit will show what Campaigner can support as it is.

  • Signup recovery that branches by audience and by what the person was trying to buy.
  • Onboarding education: how to open a business bank account with the new address, how mail handling works, how to set up the phone line.
  • Usage-based messages that show clients what they are getting, and what the next plan adds.
  • A retention sequence that starts well ahead of the one-year mark.
  • Win-back campaigns for past clients, and a referral program for the channel that already works.
