01 — What we discussed

A good first conversation, mostly about where the current setup isn't keeping up.

  • /01

    A decade under someone else's umbrella

    Sony acquired Altair in 2016, and the company operated as Sony Semiconductor Israel for years, though the product brand quietly stayed "Altair" throughout. This past April, Altair bought itself back to independence, with Sony and Pitango among the investors backing the move.

  • /02

    Not a startup, not an identity crisis

    Ilana was clear this isn't a rebrand story. Altair has an established footprint, competes directly with Qualcomm, and sells chips to module makers like Telit and Quectel, who build the boards that end device vendors use across healthcare, smart metering, smart cities, and wearables.

  • /03

    What does Ilana need?

    Now that Altair operates on its own, Ilana wants stronger brand awareness to match that independence, more and better social content, a real lifecycle marketing program, and the ability to run paid ads when it makes sense. AI visibility and GEO tracking is the most pressing of the group. She doesn't feel her current agency has the tooling to track it properly.

  • /04

    Altair marketing is mostly ABM marketing

    When account-based marketing came up, Ilana clarified that her team's targeting is tightly focused on their ICP, module makers and the end device vendors they serve, rather than a formal, campaign-based ABM program.

  • /05

    Buyers are starting to ask AI, not search engines

    Module makers and device vendors researching connectivity chips are increasingly asking LLMs directly rather than working through a search results page. If Altair isn't structured and cited the way those models look for answers, it's invisible at exactly the moment someone is evaluating vendors, regardless of how strong the product is.

  • /06

    What's already working, and what's out of scope

    LinkedIn is the real focus, roughly two posts a week. Instagram covers employer branding, Facebook was closed about a year ago. A newsletter goes out roughly monthly. Website work is explicitly not needed, and Altair's existing global PR relationship stays separate from this engagement.

03 — Where we can help

Four areas that map to what you're actually short on right now.

CAPABILITY / 01

AI Visibility & GEO

Built with proper tracking from day one, not bolted on later.

GEO runs alongside traditional SEO: the same discipline of knowing which queries matter, applied to how LLMs surface and cite vendors. This is the piece you flagged as most urgent, and the one your current agency isn't tracking well.

  • Build a target query set, the GEO equivalent of keyword research, scoped to how buyers actually ask about connectivity chips and module selection
  • Reverse-engineer the content and placements needed to earn citations against that query set
  • Evaluate Reddit and similar high-citation sources as premium placements, not community-building exercises
  • Technical site-health work that underpins both classic SEO and GEO performance
  • Ongoing tracking and reporting, so visibility is measured, not assumed

  • A mix of static and video content, in-house creative rather than a static-only feed
  • Employer branding support on Instagram, kept distinct from the LinkedIn product narrative
  • Content that translates technical credibility for a non-engineer audience without dumbing it down

Credible voices your buyers already trust, not influencers.

In a market this technical, the people who move opinion are engineers, technical leads at partner module makers, and speakers at the trade shows Altair already attends. That's a different kind of KOL program than a consumer brand runs, and it's one we already operate.

  • Identify relevant voices across module maker partners, technical press, and conference circuits
  • Produce co-branded content: technical panels, joint case studies, on-site interviews at trade shows
  • Extend into award submissions and co-presented speaking slots alongside partners like Telit and Quectel
  • Brief and manage KOLs the way we already do for clients like Lumenis, from outreach through content capture

05 — How the first three months look

Real discovery before anything goes live, then a fast ramp.

This isn't a rebrand with a launch date. The first month is deliberately slow: we need to actually understand Altair's product lines, ICP, and competitive set before publishing anything with your name on it. Work starts going live in weeks four through six.

01

Weeks 1–2 — Onboarding & deep discovery

Working sessions with your team, not just Ilana, to understand Altair's product lines, module maker relationships, ICP, and how engineers there talk about the work. We're learning the business before we touch the brand.

02

Weeks 2–3 — Brand, competitive & GEO audit

The audit becomes the working brief: current messaging, the competitive landscape against Qualcomm and adjacent players, and a first pass at Altair's AI-visibility footprint and target query set.

03

Weeks 3–4 — Content pillars & creative direction

Pillars, tone, and visual direction get built and signed off with your team before anything is scheduled to publish. Nothing goes live untested.

04

Weeks 4–6 — Work goes live

Social and content publishing starts, GEO foundations go in, and lifecycle and paid layer in as scope allows. By week six, Altair is publishing on a real plan, not catching up to one.
