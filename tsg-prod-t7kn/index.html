02 · Strategy rationale

Four emails. Only one of them is cold.

01

Israel is an opt-in jurisdiction

Section 30A of the Communications Law (Amendment 40) permits one message to a business address for the purpose of requesting consent to send more. Statutory damages run up to NIS 1,000 per message with no proof of damage required. So Email 1 asks permission, and a reply is what opens Emails 2–4. Non-repliers exit the flow and stay covered by Google search and the bot track.

02

This list cannot send from HubSpot marketing email

HubSpot's Anti-Spam Policy §2.1.1 prohibits contacting recipients from "purchased, rented, borrowed, or other third-party lists, including email append services or enrichment services lists." Enriched contacts are named explicitly. Sending the cold wave from portal 147248395 risks the same portal that runs the nurture flow. Send the cold wave from a dedicated cold-email platform on a separate sending domain, and sync only consented contacts into HubSpot.

03

72 of the 200 companies buy manufacturing

Your sheet tags 128 of 200 (64%) as non-manufacturer, and 32 of those names read as aerial-photography, surveying or media-services shops. They do not buy turnkey production. Wave 1 goes to the 72 companies tagged manufacturer, integrator, sensor and payload, C-UAS, or C2 and UTM. Einat's own suggestion of a lookalike extension is the better volume lever than diluting the send.

Email 1 · Day 0 · cold

Permission

Purpose
Establish relevance in three lines and ask one yes-or-no question. This is the single lawful cold touch, and the consent gate for everything after it.
Content
Name the moment in their language: moving from three units to serial production, or an RFP that asks for documentation and training they don't have. One line that TSG runs three production sites in Israel and works only in defense-tech robotics and autonomous platforms. No capability list. No attachment.
Subject A
scaling past the first 3 units
Subject B
documentation for your next RFP
Tone
Peer to peer. Plain, lowercase subject line, sent from a named TSG engineer rather than from marketing.
CTA
Reply-based. "Worth sending you the production capability overview?"
Button
No button · reply only
Tech note
Every token carries a tested fallback, and a missing first_name suppresses the contact rather than sending a generic greeting. Sending domain is separate from the corporate domain, with its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC, warmed to 30–40 sends a day before wave 1.

The RFP wall

Purpose

A 15-person company cannot answer it. That gap, not the technology, is what loses the contract. Close on the value chain: TSG covers R&D, DFM with full manufacturing documentation, prototype, serial production, QC, integration and global delivery.

Email 4 · Day 15 · consented only

The open door

Purpose
One direct ask with an explicit release valve. In the existing sequence the highest reply rate, 7.5%, came from its lowest-pressure email. The audience showed where it prefers to answer.
Content
Two short paragraphs. Where are you on the value chain, and what is the next thing that has to scale. Then the release valve: if the timing isn't right, no problem, we'll be here when the volume arrives.
Subject A
where are you on the chain?
Subject B
closing the loop
Tone
Low pressure, short, from a named human. No urgency language.
CTA
Direct → conversation. A 30-minute production feasibility review, or a visit to a TSG site.
Button
Book 30 minutes or come see a line
Tech note
The site visit is the differentiated ask and both parties are in Israel. A plant walkthrough is a lower-commitment, higher-signal meeting than a video call, and no offshore competitor can offer it. Worth testing as the primary CTA against the 30-minute call.
