01

Electreon sells into both B2B and B2G.

Fleet operators, OEMs and transport partners on one side; municipalities, transport authorities and public agencies on the other. The same technology, two very different buying conversations. Any marketing program has to speak to both without splitting the brand in two.

02

The priority is quick wins and qualified lead generation.

Not a 12-month brand exercise. The work needs to show movement early and feed sales with leads that are worth a conversation. That shaped which programs we put forward in this brief and how we sequenced them.

03

LinkedIn content and page management is the center of gravity.

Electreon's in-house writer, who has carried content, white papers and reports, is leaving. The LinkedIn page needs an owner who can keep the cadence going and keep it technically credible. This is where you asked us to focus, and it is where this brief goes deepest.

04

ABM, webinars and creator content were also on the table.

Account-based marketing on LinkedIn with an Influ2 or 6sense intent layer. A webinar program aimed at the people who evaluate charging infrastructure. And UGC-style video as a format that can make a very technical product feel approachable. We cover each below.

05

Lead generation has its own owner at Electreon.

You mentioned a colleague who leads lead generation and that you would check their interest in working with an outside team. There may also be a need in the UK. We have scoped lead generation here so that conversation has something concrete to react to.

06

Remote collaboration has to work from day one.

Electreon's product is engineering-led and detail-heavy, so an outside team needs to plug into how your people actually work. We have run every one of our client relationships remotely since 2010, and we describe how that works for a technical team later in this brief.

02 · The frame

Depth is the advantage, not the obstacle.

Most B2B companies struggle to find something worth saying every week. Electreon has the opposite problem. Every deployment, every kilometer of road, every test with a 40-ton truck is a story that no competitor can tell. The job is not to simplify the technology. It is to publish it, consistently, in a way that both of your buyers can act on.

Two buyers, one feed
Buyer A / B2B

Fleets, OEMs and operators

They buy on total cost of ownership, uptime and integration risk. They want to see the technology working in conditions like theirs: a bus route, a logistics corridor, a port. Content for this buyer is evidence-led: deployment updates, performance data, partner stories, engineering explainers.

01 Service 01 · Primary

LinkedIn page management & content

A technical company page that posts like it has something to say. Because it does.

We take over the Electreon LinkedIn page as its editorial team: strategy, calendar, writing, design, video editing, publishing, community management and reporting. The output is a consistent, technically accurate stream of content that your sales team is proud to share and your two buyers can each find themselves in.

Technical accuracy is the non-negotiable. We build a review loop with your engineering and marcom team so that nothing goes out that an engineer would wince at. The writing is ours; the facts stay yours.

  • Content strategy and pillars. A defined set of recurring themes mapped to your two buyers, with a monthly calendar you approve in advance.
  • Writing and design. Posts, carousels, short explainers, deployment updates and project milestones, written by a B2B writer and designed in your visual language.
  • Video and creator-style content. Edited clips from site footage, interviews with your engineers and partners, and UGC-style walk-throughs that make the technology feel tangible (examples below).
  • Events as content engines. Your marcom coordinator finds the events; we turn each one into weeks of posts before, during and after.
  • Executive voice. Optional ghostwriting for Electreon leaders so the company page and personal profiles reinforce each other.
  • Community management and reporting. Daily monitoring and replies, monthly reporting on reach, engagement, follower growth and which themes move each buyer.
Cadence

3 to 4 posts a week

A steady weekly rhythm across formats. Enough to compound, not so much that quality slips or the feed feels automated.

Formats

Text, carousel, video, document

Mixed on purpose. LinkedIn rewards format variety, and your buyers consume differently: engineers read documents, officials watch 60 seconds of video.

Review

Technical sign-off built in

Every piece passes through a named reviewer on your side before it is scheduled. We hold a shared calendar so nothing surprises anyone.

Reporting

Monthly, by buyer

We report on what B2B and B2G audiences each respond to, not just totals, and adjust the calendar accordingly.

02 Service 02

Lead generation: paid media & ABM

Electreon does not need everyone. It needs the right few hundred organizations to raise their hand.

Electreon's market is finite and knowable: a defined set of fleet operators, OEMs, transport authorities, cities and infrastructure owners. That is the textbook case for account-based marketing rather than broad demand generation. We build the account list with you, layer intent data on it, and run LinkedIn and search campaigns that reach the buying committee inside each account, not the general public.

This is the program we would scope with your lead generation lead, and it extends naturally to the UK if that need materializes: same system, a second account list.

  • Tiered account list. Built with your sales team: Tier 1 named accounts, Tier 2 look-alikes, Tier 3 the wider addressable market. Separate lists for B2B and B2G.
  • Intent layer. Influ2 for person-level targeting of named buying committees, or 6sense for account-level intent signals. We recommend based on list size and budget.
  • LinkedIn campaigns. Document ads, thought-leader ads and conversation ads built from the organic content program, so paid and organic compound.
  • Search capture. Google Search for the high-intent terms your buyers actually use when they start evaluating, plus brand defense.
  • Landing pages and lead routing. Purpose-built pages per buyer, with lead scoring and handoff into your CRM so sales sees quality, not volume.
  • Attribution. Reporting at the account level: which named organizations engaged, how deep, and which converted to a meeting.

03 Service 03

Webinar program

Forty-five minutes with your engineers is worth more than forty ads. The trick is filling the room and following up.

For a technology that has to be understood before it can be bought, a webinar is the conversion moment. It is where a fleet director or a transport planner hears directly from the people who built the system, asks the hard questions, and leaves with a reason to take a meeting. We run the program end to end so your team only has to show up and present.

Each session also becomes content: clips for LinkedIn, a gated on-demand version for lead capture, and a follow-up sequence per audience.

  • Program design. A six-session arc with topics mapped to your buyers and your deployment calendar, co-presenters sourced from partners, cities and operators where possible.
  • Promotion. LinkedIn organic and paid, email to your database and ours, partner co-promotion. Registration pages built and tracked.
  • Production. Platform setup, speaker prep, run-of-show, live moderation, polls and Q&A management.
  • Follow-up. Segmented sequences for attendees, no-shows and on-demand viewers, with leads routed to sales with context.
  • Reporting. Registrations, live attendance, engagement, leads and meetings per session, with the program improving session over session.

03 · How we work with a technical team

Remote since 2010. Embedded by design.

Noetic has been remote-first since the day it was founded. Our team of about 40 works across the Americas and Israel, and every client relationship we have ever run has been run this way, including with engineering-led companies where accuracy is everything. Here is what that looks like in practice.

01

A named pod, not a rotating cast

An account lead, a writer, a designer and a media or webinar specialist assigned to Electreon. The same people every week, in your Slack or Teams, on your time zone for the working day.

02

Technical onboarding first

Before the first post, a structured immersion: product briefings with your engineers, a glossary we maintain, a reading list of your white papers and reports, and a list of what never gets said.

03

A review loop you control

A shared content calendar with named reviewers on your side. Every piece has a status you can see. Nothing publishes without sign-off, and turnaround expectations are agreed up front so review never becomes a bottleneck.

04

Weekly rhythm, monthly depth

A short weekly working session for the calendar and anything live. A monthly review with reporting by buyer, what we learned and what changes next month. Quarterly, a strategy reset.
