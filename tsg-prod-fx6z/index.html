02 · Before the first send

Three things to solve first.
Each one has a fix.

Current stateWhat we do about it
01

Israel is an opt-in jurisdiction

Section 30A of the Communications Law permits one message to a business address, and only if its purpose is to request consent for further contact. Statutory damages run up to NIS 1,000 per message with no proof of damage required. Rules vary by country and Israel sits among the stricter regimes.

The fix

Email 1 does one job: it asks permission. A reply is what opens Emails 2 to 4. Non-repliers exit the flow and stay covered by Google search and the bot track.

02

This list cannot send from HubSpot

HubSpot's Anti-Spam Policy §2.1.1 names enrichment-sourced lists among those you may not contact through HubSpot. That is a terms rule, not a missing feature or a permission that can be requested. Portal 147248395 also runs the existing nurture, so an action against the cold wave would take that flow down with it.

The fix

Email 1 sends from a purpose-built cold-email platform on a separate warmed domain. Once a contact replies, consent is documented and Emails 2 to 4 run inside HubSpot as normal.

Additional cost · Instantly $47/mo or Smartlead $39/mo entry plan · TSG
03

The 200 are company names, not contacts

No verified contact exists for the list today. Roughly a third carries a manufacturer, integrator, sensor and payload, C-UAS, or command-and-control tag; the rest needs a fit pass against production as well as manufacturing before it is worth sending to.

The fix

Source first, then extend. The tool on the previous slide turns the 200 into contacts, and a lookalike extension, as Einat proposed, adds volume without diluting the send into non-buyers.

Israeli legal figures come from law-firm commentary on Section 30A, not from primary statute text. TSG counsel signs off on the consent mechanic before the first send.

03 · The flow · Emails 1 and 2

Email 1 asks. Email 2 introduces.

Email 1 · Day 0 · cold

Permission

Purpose
Establish relevance in three lines and ask one yes-or-no question. This is the single lawful cold touch, and the consent gate for everything after it.
Content
Name the moment in their language: moving from three units to serial production, or an RFP asking for documentation and training they do not have. Capabilities named at a high level in one line, three production sites in Israel, defense-tech robotics and autonomous platforms only. No capability list, no attachment.
Tone
Peer to peer. Plain lowercase subject line, sent from a named TSG engineer rather than from marketing.
CTA
Reply-based. "Worth sending you more on how we work?"
Button
No button · reply only
Tech note
Every token carries a tested fallback, and a missing first_name suppresses the contact rather than sending a generic greeting. Sending domain separate from the corporate domain, with its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC, warmed to 30–40 sends a day before wave 1.
Email 2 · Day 4 · consented only

Focus over features

Purpose
Show who TSG is before naming any problem. Turnkey manufacturing is not proprietary, so the differentiator is focus and track record, stated plainly.
Content
TSG does not do medical and does not do pharma. Defense-tech, autonomous platforms and robotics only. 50+ years, three production sites in Israel, delivered to the IDF, Home Front Command and global air forces. One worked example along the chain, from system architecture through serial production to global delivery.
Tone
Confident and concrete. No superlatives, no "best-in-class", no pitch.
CTA
Light → proof. A production-site walkthrough or one customer journey.
Button
See a line in operation
Tech note
This is the email that needs an asset from TSG. A site video or one anonymised customer journey. The DroneWeaver videos do not transfer, since this is a different audience and reusing them would break message match. If nothing lands in time, Email 2 runs on the written focus argument alone.
03 · The flow · Emails 3 and 4

Email 3 names the problem. Email 4 opens the door.

Email 3 · Day 9 · consented only

The RFP wall

Purpose
Name the pain that makes the offering urgent, now that the reader knows who TSG is, and deliver the capability asset.
Content
Einat's RFP story, generalised. You win the deal, and then the buyer's RFP asks for manufacturing documentation, training material, in-country support and a quality system. A 15-person company cannot answer it. That gap, not the technology, is what loses the contract. Close on the value chain: R&D, DFM with full manufacturing documentation, prototype, serial production, QC, integration and global delivery.
Tone
Diagnostic and specific. Describes their situation before describing TSG.
CTA
Moderate → content. Production capability overview: concept to delivery.
Button
Get the capability overview
Tech note
The production offering is not on the website and does not arrive until the October redesign. The flow does not wait for it. The asset is a standalone one-page capability overview built from the production chain Einat presented, so wave 1 can launch before the redesign.
Email 4 · Day 15 · consented only

The open door

Purpose
One direct ask with an explicit release valve. In the existing sequence the highest reply rate, 7.5%, came from its lowest-pressure email. The audience showed where it prefers to answer.
Content
Two short paragraphs. Where are you on the value chain, and what is the next thing that has to scale. Then the release valve: if the timing is not right, no problem, we will be here when the volume arrives.
Tone
Low pressure, short, from a named human. No urgency language.
CTA
Direct → conversation. A 30-minute production feasibility review, or a visit to a TSG site.
Button
Book 30 minutes or come see a line
Tech note
The site visit is the differentiated ask and both parties are in Israel. A plant walkthrough is a lower-commitment, higher-signal meeting than a video call, and no offshore competitor can offer it. Worth testing as the primary CTA against the 30-minute call.
