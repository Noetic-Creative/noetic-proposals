01

The goal is lead generation in the United States

Tadbik already has a sales presence in the US. What it does not yet have is a steady flow of new inbound conversations for that team to work. The brief is to create that flow, in the US only, with a focus on quality over volume.

02

Paid search is the right place to start

We talked through where intent lives. Paid search reaches buyers at the moment they are looking for a packaging or labeling solution. Social channels reach people whose intent is undefined. For a first US program with a clear lead-gen goal, search is the channel that pays back first.

03

Tadbik is starting fresh on Google Ads

There is no existing Google Ads account and no past campaign history in the US. That means no legacy structure to untangle, and it means the first weeks will be about research and setup rather than optimization.

04

Landing pages matter as much as the ads

We spent time on this one. In B2B packaging the click is only half the work. Where the buyer lands, what they read and whether it speaks to their industry decides if a visit becomes a lead. We discussed dedicated landing pages rather than sending traffic to the main website.

05

Customization by vertical, not one message for everyone

Tadbik serves food and beverage, personal care, pharma, industrial and more, across labels, sleeves, flexible packaging, IML and smart labeling. We discussed building the search program and the landing pages per vertical, so a beverage brand manager and a pharma packaging engineer each see a page written for them.

06

Lifecycle and email as the second engine

We touched on what happens after the lead arrives. Email and lifecycle automation keep the conversation going with buyers who are not ready to speak to sales yet, which in a long-cycle category is most of them. We agreed this is a natural next layer once search is running.

07

Budget ranges

We discussed a suggested monthly media investment and a suggested monthly retainer for campaign management and landing pages. Both are summarized in the investment section of this brief, with email and lifecycle work scoped separately.

02 · The frame

Tadbik makes the label fit the package. We make the campaign fit the buyer.

Tadbik's whole business is customization: the right material, the right adhesive, the right print for one product on one line under one set of rules. Generic paid search is the opposite of that. In a low-volume, high-intent category, the campaign and the landing page have to be built per vertical, the same way Tadbik builds per customer.

Intent

Search first, because the buyer raised their hand

A US packaging buyer typing "shrink sleeve supplier" or "pharmaceutical booklet labels" is telling you what they need. Paid search is the only channel where that happens at scale.

Fit

One landing page per vertical, not one website for all

The main site has to serve every market Tadbik works in. A landing page can serve one. It can use that buyer's vocabulary, show that buyer's applications and answer that buyer's compliance questions.

Proof

We have run this exact play for Israeli manufacturers

TIPA (packaging) and Hazera (seeds) were both niche, both Israeli, both selling into markets where the buyer's words differed from the company's. Both grew leads while spend went down or stayed flat.

Two ways to run US paid search
The common approach

One campaign. One page. Everyone.

Broad keywords like "packaging solutions" and "labels manufacturer," ads that list capabilities, traffic sent to the homepage. It feels efficient. It produces clicks from students, job seekers and small print shops, and the US sales team learns to ignore the leads.

The Noetic approach

One campaign per vertical. One page per buyer.

Keyword research per industry, in the buyer's language. Ads that name the application, not the capability. A landing page that opens with the problem that vertical has (compliance, shelf impact, line speed, sustainability) and closes with the proof Tadbik already owns.

Hazera

Featured · Israeli manufacturer, niche B2B
Agriculture · Seed development · Paid Search + Landing Pages
Challenge

Hazera, one of Israel's leading seed developers, wanted to introduce new products into markets where it already operated. The buyer persona was extremely specific and search volume was small, leaving no room for broad or generic campaigns.

Method

We studied the grower personas and the competitive landscape, then built one campaign system that tied each product to its crop, its region and the specific proof a grower looks for. Ads and landing pages moved from problem (resistance) to proof (shelf life) to outcome (yield), leading with product value rather than brand.

03 · Paid search
01Service 01
Paid Search

Built for the buyer who is already looking.

In B2B packaging the search universe is small and the stakes per lead are high. Our paid search practice is designed for exactly that: decode how the buyer searches, structure the account by vertical and application, write ads that sound like the industry, and protect every dollar of a modest budget from the clicks that do not matter.

  • Keyword research per vertical, in the buyer's vocabulary, not the catalog's
  • Account structured by vertical and application, so budget and learnings stay clean
  • Tight negative keyword lists from day one (consumer, jobs, DIY, local print shops)
  • Ad copy that names the application and the compliance context, not just the capability
  • Conversion tracking and a shared dashboard so Tadbik sees every lead and its source
04 · Landing pages
02Service 02
Landing Pages, Personalized by Vertical

Where the click becomes a conversation.

The Tadbik website has to speak to every market at once. A landing page only has to speak to one. We design and build a dedicated page per vertical, each opening with that buyer's problem, showing that buyer's applications, and asking for the conversation in that buyer's terms. The pages live on Tadbik's domain, carry Tadbik's brand, and are built to be tested and improved month over month.

How each page is built
The problemThe applicationThe proofThe ask
  • One page per launch vertical (4 to 6 to start), written in the buyer's vocabulary
  • Structure: the problem, the application, the proof, the ask
  • Compliance and sustainability cues placed where that vertical expects them
  • Short, qualifying forms routed to the US sales team
  • Heatmaps and A/B tests on headline, proof and form each month

06 · The second engine

Search brings them in. Lifecycle keeps them warm.

In packaging, most buyers who raise a hand are not ready to place an order. They are scoping a project, comparing suppliers or waiting on a budget cycle. Email and lifecycle automation keep Tadbik present during that gap, so when the project is real, the US sales team is already the familiar name. We would scope this as its own workstream once search is live.

  1. Step 01

    Capture

    Every landing page lead is tagged by vertical and application at the moment it arrives.

  2. Step 02

    Route

    Sales-ready leads go to the US team the same day. The rest enter a nurture track for their vertical.

  3. Step 03

    Nurture

    Short, useful emails per vertical: application guides, compliance updates, ECO LINE options, case examples.

  4. Step 04

    Resurface

    Engagement signals (opens, clicks, return visits) alert sales when a quiet lead becomes an active one.
