01 · What we talked about

A quick record of the conversation.

  • /01

    The American side is a different market

    Israir is putting two A330 widebodies on Tel Aviv to New York, with up to six flights a week. Half of those seats have to be sold to people sitting in the United States, and that is a very different marketing job from selling in Israel.

  • /02

    Five communities, plus business travel

    Israeli-Americans, Jewish-Americans, Jewish-Latin families, Orthodox families, and pro-Israel Christian and Evangelical travelers. Each one needs its own message, its own talking points, its own call to action and its own landing page. On top of that sits business travel.

  • /03

    A second lane: the companies in the middle

    You mentioned the Israeli-American companies whose people are on this route constantly. Those are not single tickets, they are annual volume. Reaching them is an account based motion, not an advertising one.

  • /04

    AI visibility is the gap you named

    You said it directly. An Israeli agency cannot really do American SEO and AI visibility, because it is a different search culture with different communities and different sources. This came up as the clearest opening for us.

  • /05

    What happens after the booking

    Someone buys a ticket today. The next six months of email, offers and news should already be written and waiting. That is a CRM and lifecycle job, and it is one of the cheapest ways to get a second booking.

02 · The job in front of you

You are not just opening a route. You are launching a brand in a new country.

In Israel, everyone knows Israir. In the United States, almost nobody does. That is the real distance to cover, and it is a marketing problem more than an aviation one. Here is what the market looks like today.

Share of Israeli carrier traffic on United States routes

El Al’s share of the United States market grew from 75.7 percent to 92.3 percent over the past year, while foreign carriers stayed away. Almost every American flying to Tel Aviv on an Israeli airline right now is flying El Al, largely out of habit. Habits are the thing marketing can change. Source: Israeli carrier second quarter results, August 2026.

When the rest of the competition comes back

United and Delta have suspended their Tel Aviv service until at least September, and American until January. The route is unusually quiet for a launch. Every month spent building recognition now is a month of advantage later.

01

Be known

Most Americans booking this route have never seen the Israir name. Before anyone compares your price, they have to believe you are a real, safe, established airline. That is awareness work, and it takes months, not weeks.

02

Give a reason

Being cheaper is not enough on a route people care about. Each community needs its own reason to choose you, and the reason that convinces an Orthodox family is not the one that convinces a church group.

03

Come back

These are repeat travelers. Most fly this route again within a year. Winning someone once is worth far more if the second and third booking are already being worked on from the day they land.

SERVICE / 02

SEO & AI Visibility

When someone asks an AI how to fly to Tel Aviv, you should be in the answer.

This is the part you said your Israeli agency cannot really help with, and we agree. American search is its own world. People ask ChatGPT, they read Reddit threads, they check DansDeals, they trust community forums more than airline websites. Being visible there is now as important as ranking on Google.

  • Understand what AI assistants currently say about Israir, then work to improve it
  • English language SEO for the route, the destination and the practical questions people ask
  • Presence in the places these communities actually read: Reddit, travel forums, community sites and deal blogs
  • Content shaped as answers, because that is what AI systems pull from
  • Clean technical foundations: structured data, fast pages, correct information everywhere
  • Monthly monitoring, so you can see visibility moving rather than guessing

Lifecycle & CRM

Someone books today. The next six months are already written.

Most airlines send a confirmation and then go quiet until the flight. That is a lot of wasted attention. We would run your CRM and build the whole arc: before the flight, during the trip, after they get home, and into the next booking.

  • CRM setup and clean-up, so the data is usable and segmented from day one
  • A six month flow that starts the moment someone books
  • Pre-flight practical emails: baggage, kosher meals, timing, what to expect on board
  • Post-return sequences that lead into the next trip and the next holiday
  • Promotions timed to each community's calendar, not one generic newsletter
  • Language and content branching per segment, all from one system

SERVICE / 05

ABM for the corporate corridor

The people flying this route twenty times a year all work somewhere.

This one runs completely differently. Instead of advertising to an audience, we build a named list of companies with real presence in both countries, then reach the specific people who decide where their teams fly. The good news is that this market is already measured, and it is bigger than most people assume.

  • Build the named account list: Israeli-American companies with genuine two way traffic
  • Identify the real decision makers, usually travel, office or HR leads
  • LinkedIn and person level targeting against that named list only
  • A corporate landing page and inquiry flow built for this audience
  • Direct outreach and offline touches, because these lists are small enough to do properly
  • Simple reporting on accounts reached, meetings booked and volume won

04 · Beyond the feed

Three things that still work.

These communities are tight and physical. They live in specific neighborhoods, read specific papers and trust people they know. Some old channels work very well here.

01

Direct mail

Postal targeting by neighborhood is precise and cheap in the United States. A well designed piece landing in Brooklyn, Lakewood, Bal Harbour or Great Neck before a holiday season is a real channel, not a nostalgia play.

02

Billboards and out of home

Highly visible, community by community. Roads into the airport, main streets in the neighborhoods that matter, and community newspapers. It also gives the brand credibility, which matters when you are new on the route.

03

A referral program

These communities talk to each other constantly. A simple, well built referral offer turns that into bookings. We can design it, build it, connect it to the CRM and run it.
