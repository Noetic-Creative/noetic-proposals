Method

Attribution methodology and caveats

ID-tagged leads (from Sep 16). utm_campaign holds the Google campaign ID, utm_term holds the ad group ID, and utm_content holds the keyword. These match the ad report exactly.

Static tags (before Sep 16). These use utm_campaign=B2B&utm_term=TB plus a landing-page tag. brandedTBNew maps to Branded. TeamBuildingNYC_newLP maps to Team Building In NYC. teambuildingeventsfun_newLP and teambuildingeventsmanhattan_newLP map to Fun Building Activities – Manhattan/NYC. The campaign match is reliable. The ad-group split is an educated guess.

Performance Max. The report gives one combined PMax figure ($5,273). Leads tagged B2B_Remarketing / pmax and B2C_Shop / remarketing sit under it with no separate spend. Campaign 23070915554 and the literal {campaign} tag are assumed to be B2C Shop.

Search spend outside ad-group rows. Search totals $39,371, but the enabled ad groups total $35,984. The $3,387 gap (326 clicks, 6 conversions) belongs to ad groups no longer in the report. No leads match it.

Google conversions vs. CRM leads. Google counts conversion actions: form fills, calls, and B2C purchases. The CRM counts people. A ratio well above 1× means the platform counts events that never became a lead record.

B2C revenue. Google reports $4,758 in B2C Search conversion value and $2,780 in PMax value, from online purchases. Online buyers rarely become CRM leads, so B2C Search looks weaker here than it is.

Filters. The client-type filter changes only the CRM columns. Google doesn't split spend by B2B and B2C, so cost per lead under a filter uses the campaign's full spend.

Exclusions. Two test leads from the Noetic domain are removed. The Meta files are not included, so the 8 Meta leads show results with no cost.
