Webinar Performance Report · Prepared for Sapiens

We reached exactly the right people. Almost none of them registered.

"The Reinsurance Premium You Don't Know You're Paying" put a data-quality message about MGA reinsurance in front of a precisely targeted audience of finance leaders. The promotional emails found their mark: of the people who opened them, 99% held the exact titles in the brief. Registration did not follow. Three people registered, one attended, and he watched almost the entire session. This report is about the gap between a correct audience and a converting one, and what it tells us about the topic and the ask.

Session · August 20, 2026 · 10:00 AM ET / 3:00 PM BST Format · Pre-recorded, 20 minutes including Q&A Speaker · Darren Graves, Sapiens · with LTM

/01

The audience was not wrong. The conversion was.

Of the 191 people who opened the promotional emails, 189 held one of the three brief-specified titles: 93 Controllers, 84 CFOs and 12 in reinsurance roles. Recognizable carriers were in the open list, including Zurich American, Arch Insurance, RLI, Sentry and Allianz Global Risks. Targeting delivered the right inboxes. The problem sits downstream of the open, at the decision to give up twenty minutes.

/02

Opens were healthy for a cold send. Registrations were not.

The three promotional sends opened at 5.24%, 5.17% and 4.07% on delivered mail, in the normal band for a cold B2B sequence to this seniority. But 299 total opens produced only 3 registrations, roughly a 1% open-to-register rate. People were willing to open a provocative subject line and unwilling to commit calendar time.

/03

The one person who came, stayed.

Scott Haynes of Southern Oak Insurance joined nine seconds after the open and left at 10:17:43, 17.6 minutes into a 20-minute session. That is 88% of the content. The single data point we have on engagement is strongly positive: the person who chose to attend found it worth nearly all of his time.

/04

The shortfall showed up early, and we intervened.

When registrations stayed flat through the standard promotional cycle, the program did not coast to the session date. A second list of 2,666 contacts was requested from Sapiens and added mid-flight, and a last-minute reminder was pushed to everyone who had merely opened a promotional email. Section 04 documents these efforts in full. They are the reason this report exists as a study rather than a shrug.

/05

The most likely cause is the ask, not the message.

Unsubscribes stayed near zero across every send, which means the content did not repel anyone. Combined with an on-target open list, the pattern points to topic pull and format demand rather than list quality or creative. A CFO will open an email about a hidden reinsurance cost. Asking that same CFO to book twenty minutes on a data-governance webinar is a materially larger request.

Section 04 · The demand engine

What we did when the numbers came in low.

When registration came in below expectation, the program did not coast to the session date. It responded with three deliberate moves outside the standard playbook, two before the webinar and one after. Each is documented below so the effort is legible even though the outcome was not what we wanted.

01Before

Audience expansion

We asked Sapiens for more reach, and got it.

When registrations stalled after the initial promotional list, we requested more audience from the Sapiens team and received a second list of 2,666 contacts. Its first send went out the day before the session. There was no time for the added audience to move through a normal consideration cycle, so its contribution to registrations was structurally limited, but it materially widened the top of the funnel.

2,666Extra contacts added mid-flight, sent the day before the webinar
02Before

The open-based last-minute push

We put the join link in front of everyone who had shown interest.

Normally the five-minute reminder with the direct join link goes only to registrants. Here we extended it to 185 contacts who had opened a promotional email but never registered, converting warm interest at the last possible moment. Of those, 61 opened and 5 clicked. That effort is the most probable reason the one live attendee ended up in the room.

185Non-registrant openers given the direct join link · 61 opened, 5 clicked
03After

The recording as a fallback channel

"We're sorry we missed you," sent past the usual boundary.

Because the session lives on-demand on the Sapiens site, we sent the "Sorry we missed you" email with the video to everyone who had opened a promotional email, not only to registrants as we normally would. The goal was to salvage value from a well-produced 20 minutes by putting it in front of the interested-but-unconverted, on their own schedule, where the twenty-minute ask becomes a click-whenever-you-want ask.

The audience was correct and interested but would not commit to a live slot. An on-demand asset removes exactly that friction, so on-demand views are the number to watch from here.

Section 05 · Voice of the room

The room was too small to have a voice.

Three survey questions were prepared for this session: how relevant the material was, whether it met expectations, and whether attendees would return for future Sapiens webinars. With a single live attendee and no responses submitted in the export, there is no survey data and no Q&A to report. That absence is itself the finding, and it points back at registration rather than at the content.

Section 06 · Methodology

How every number here was produced, and where the deck disagrees.

This report recomputes from source rather than restating the deck. Three figures in the source materials do not reconcile cleanly; each is handled explicitly below rather than smoothed over.

Registrations: 3

The raw Registrants tab lists exactly three people, all tagged "hs_automation email": Marina Francisco (MIEC), Scott Haynes (Southern Oak) and Brett Hoke (Root). The deck's Overview slide agrees at 3. The deck's separate "Source of Registrations" slide shows Email 4 and Direct 4, which totals 8 and cannot be reconciled with the other two. We anchor on the raw sheet and report 3, confirmed as the source of truth.

Cold audience size: unique contacts, not 4,125

The deck states an initial list of 2,447 and an expansion of 2,666, then a "Total Enrolled" of 4,125. Those two lists sum to 5,113, not 4,125, so 4,125 is neither their sum nor any single send. We therefore describe reach in terms of the actual per-send volumes (2,971, then 1,792, then 1,781 to re-engaged segments) and treat 4,125 as an approximate unique-contact figure after de-duplication and suppression, which is the only framing consistent with two overlapping lists. The 4,125 is retained here only as that footnoted estimate.

Attendance: 1 of 3

The Zoom attendee export lists two "Presenter" accounts, which are the simulive playback streams for a pre-recorded session, and one genuine attendee, Scott Haynes. Presenters are excluded from every attendance and retention figure. Attendance rate is 1 of 3 registrations, or 33%.

Retention: 88% of content

Scott Haynes joined at 10:00:09 and left at 10:17:43, a watch time of 17.6 minutes. The brief specifies a 20-minute pre-recorded session including Q&A, so retention is 17.6 of 20, or 88%. Measured against the Zoom "actual duration" of 22.3 minutes (which includes a short tail past the content), the same watch time is 79%. We report against the 20-minute content window.

Audience quality: 191 openers

The "People who opened the promotional emails" tab holds 191 individuals with a job title. Titles were bucketed by keyword (CFO/chief financial, controller, reinsurance) and industry consolidated from the HubSpot industry and company fields. This tab is also the audience for both the 185-contact 5-minute push and the post-webinar recording send.

Email naming

The HubSpot email names carry a "Webinar #5" label, while the brief, registrant companies and session topic all confirm this is the reinsurance webinar. The label is an internal naming artifact and does not affect any figure.

Closing

The right room was reachable. The next question is how they want to meet.

This webinar under-registered, but the data is unusually legible about why. We reached the exact finance leaders the brief named, they did not treat the topic as unworthy, and the one who attended stayed almost to the end. The gap was the live, twenty-minute ask made to some of the busiest people in insurance. The expansion list, the extended last-minute reminder and the open-based recording send were the right moves against that gap, and the on-demand views are the number to watch from here. Thank you to the Sapiens team for the fast turnaround on the additional list.
